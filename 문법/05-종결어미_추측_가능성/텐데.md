## 을/ㄹ 텐데 #card
?begin
### Usage
(으)ㄹ 텐데 = (으)ㄹ 터이다 + (으)ㄴ데: "I suppose/expect that ~, (and/but) ...". The first clause is the speaker's assumption (background); the second clause gives extra information, a suggestion, advice, a question, or a contrast. There are three main uses:
1. To suppose that something is the case, as a soft/polite sentence ending (the second clause is left unsaid)
2. To suppose something and then provide extra info, a suggestion, or advice
3. To suppose something and then give a contrast (the actual result differs from the expectation)

It can also express supposition about a future event, or about something that did not happen ("it would have been nice if X had happened" — see counterfactual below), and — with 걱정이다 — worry about a situation.
### Form
#### Adjective/Verb + -(으)ㄹ 텐데 (present/future) = would, suppose, must be...
* 작다 → 작을 텐데 (must be small)
* 바쁘다 → 바쁠 텐데 (must be busy)
* 가다 → 갈 텐데 (would go)
* 먹다 → 먹을 텐데 (would eat)
#### Adjective/Verb + -았/었을 텐데 (past)
* 작다 → 작았을 텐데 (would have been small)
* 가다 → 갔을 텐데 (would have gone)
* 먹다 → 먹었을 텐데 (would have eaten)
#### Noun + 일 텐데 (present) = would, suppose, must be...
* 부자 → 부자일 텐데 (must be rich)
* 학생 → 학생일 텐데 (must be a student)
#### Noun + 였을/이었을 텐데 (past)
* 부자 → 부자였을 텐데 (would have been rich)
* 학생 → 학생이었을 텐데 (would have been a student)
### Restrictions
- Cannot be used with the future tense marker (-겠-) directly.
- When using first-person subjects (the speaker's own intention), only 테니까 may be used (see [[을 테니까]]):
  * 나는 요리를 할 테니까, 너는 설거지를 해 (O)
  * 나는 요리를 할 텐데, 너는 설거지를 해 (X)
- However, when you ask someone something based on an assumption, only 텐데 may be used:
  * 배고플 텐데 라면을 끓여 줄까요? (O)
  * 배고플 테니까 라면을 끓여 줄까요? (X)
- The first clause is always background info/assumption, and the second clause is usually a suggestion, advice, a question, or a contrast.
### Examples
#### 1. Soft supposition (second clause unsaid)
* 식당이 문을 안 열었을 텐데
	* (I suppose) the restaurant wouldn't have opened...
* 영호 씨는 스페인어를 잘 못할 텐데요
	* Youngho probably can't speak Spanish well, though...
#### 2. Supposition + extra info/suggestion/advice
* 배고플 텐데 얼른 오세요
	* You must be hungry, so come quickly
* 배고플 텐데 어서 드세요.
	* You must be hungry, please eat quickly.
* 눈이 와서 길이 미끄러울 텐데 조심하세요
	* It snowed so the road is probably slippery, so be careful
* 어제 눈이 와서 길이 미끄러웠을 텐데, 잘 갔어요?
	* It snowed yesterday so the road must have been slippery — did you get there OK?
* 그곳은 너무 추울 텐데, 따뜻한 태국으로 갑시다
	* It will probably be too cold there, so let's go to warm Thailand
* 비가 올 텐데 우산을 가져가세요.
	* It's probably going to rain, so take an umbrella.
#### 3. Supposition + contrast
* 많이 아플 텐데, 울지도 않네
	* It must hurt a lot, yet (he/she) doesn't even cry
* 지수가 벌써 한국에 도착했을 텐데, 아직 연락이 없네요
	* Jisu should have already arrived in Korea, but there's still no contact
#### Counterfactual (regret / "would have been nice")
* 좀 더 일찍 왔으면 길이 안 막혔을 텐데.
	* If you had come a bit earlier, the traffic wouldn't have been so bad.
#### 을 텐데 걱정이다 (expressing worry)
Used when expressing concern about a potential situation: the speaker worries that some situation might happen, or that they will not be able to do something that they really have to do or that should be done.
* 다음 주에 중요한 출장이 있지요?
	* You have an important business trip next week, right?
	* 네, 그래서 다음 주에 아주 바쁠 텐데 걱정이에요
		* Yes, so I'll probably be very busy next week and I'm worried
* 고등학교에 가면 어려운 것을 배울 텐데 걱정이에요
	* When I go to high school I'll have to learn difficult things, and I'm worried
* 지금 출발하면 길이 많이 막힐 텐데 걱정이에요
	* If we leave now the roads will probably be very jammed, and I'm worried
* 서울에서 집을 구하면 집값이 많이 비쌀 텐데 걱정이에요
	* If I look for a house in Seoul, the prices will probably be very expensive, and I'm worried
* 다음 주에 면접을 본다면서요?
	* I heard you have an interview next week?
	* 네, 잘 봐야 할 텐데 걱정이에요
		* Yes, I need to do well, and I'm worried
* 부모님께서 다음 주에 수술을 받으신다면서요?
	* I heard your parents are having surgery next week?
	* 네, 수술이 잘 되어야 할 텐데 걱정이에요
		* Yes, the surgery needs to go well, and I'm worried
* 우리 애가 아직 밥을 못 먹었을 텐데 걱정이에요
	* My kid probably hasn't eaten yet, and I'm worried
* 그 사람이 어제 너무 바빴어요. 그래서 어제 병원에 못 갔을 텐데 걱정이에요
	* That person was too busy yesterday. So (he/she) probably couldn't go to the hospital yesterday, and I'm worried
### Notes
#### 텐데 vs 을까 봐
Both are often followed by 걱정이다/걱정했어요, but they differ:

**-(으)ㄹ 텐데** expresses the speaker's **assumption or expectation** — you are fairly confident about what you think will happen:
- 안 올 텐데 걱정했어요
	- I figured/assumed you wouldn't come, so I was worried
	- The speaker is leaning toward believing it will happen

**-(으)ㄹ까 봐** expresses **fear or worry about a possibility** — you are not sure it will happen but you are anxious about it:
- 안 올까 봐 걱정했어요
	- I was worried you might not come
	- The speaker sees it as a possibility they are nervous about, not necessarily expecting

안 올까 봐 걱정했어요 is the **more natural choice** when you were genuinely *worried*, because:
- Worry implies uncertainty and anxiety, not a firm assumption
- 텐데 carries a stronger sense of "I was pretty sure you wouldn't come", which makes worrying feel slightly contradictory — if you were that certain, the emotion would be more like disappointment than worry

In short: **텐데** = "I expected/assumed"; **을까 봐** = "I was scared/worried that maybe".
- Compared with [[을 테니까]] (which means "because"), 텐데 is more of a "so/and/but" and doesn't have to connect a reason with a consequence. If there is no consequence to the assumption, then 텐데 must be used.
