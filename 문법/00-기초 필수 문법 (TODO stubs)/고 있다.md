## 고 있다 #card
?begin
### Usage
Expresses the progressive aspect — "is/was/will be doing [verb]", an action in progress. Can also,
for certain verbs (입다, 쓰다, 신다, 타다 — verbs describing a change into a wearable/state), express
a continuing resultant state rather than an ongoing action (see Notes).
### Form
Verb stem + -고 있다 (no vowel harmony needed since 고 always attaches the same way)
가다 → 가고 있다, 먹다 → 먹고 있다
### Restrictions
- Only used with action verbs, not descriptive verbs/adjectives.
- With verbs like 입다 (wear), 신다 (wear shoes), 쓰다 (wear glasses/hat), 타다 (ride), 알다 (know),
  -고 있다 usually describes the current state/result (e.g. 옷을 입고 있어요 = "I am wearing clothes"
  right now, not necessarily "in the process of putting them on").
- Honorific form: -고 계시다 (for the subject you're showing respect to).
### Examples
* 지금 밥을 먹고 있어요.
	* I am eating right now.
* 친구가 공부하고 있어요.
	* My friend is studying.
* 어제 이 시간에 자고 있었어요.
	* I was sleeping at this time yesterday.
* 할머니께서 신문을 읽고 계세요.
	* Grandmother is reading the newspaper. (honorific)
* 그 사람은 빨간 모자를 쓰고 있어요.
	* That person is wearing a red hat. (resultant state, not "in the act of putting it on")
### Notes
Contrast with [[아 어 있다]], which more strictly emphasizes a resultant state after a completed
change (e.g. 문이 열려 있어요 — "the door is [in the state of being] open").
?end
