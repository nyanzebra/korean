## (으)ㅁ (nominalizer) #card
?begin
### Usage
Turns a verb or adjective into a noun, similar to English "-ing" or "the fact/act of ~ing". Used
especially for completed/fixed facts, formal writing, headlines, labels, and academic contexts.
Contrasts with the -기 nominalizer, which is more common for abstract, general, or future/ongoing
actions.
### Form
| Stem ends in vowel or ㄹ | Ends in other consonant |
| --- | --- |
| -ㅁ | -음 |
가다 → 감, 먹다 → 먹음, 살다 → 삶 (ㄹ + ㅁ merges)
### Restrictions
- (으)ㅁ nominalized nouns often appear in formal/written contexts: signs, notices, research
  abstracts, diaries, and certain fixed expressions (있음/없음, 죽음, 믿음, 삶, 꿈).
- -기 tends to be preferred for actions that are ongoing, hypothetical, or paired with certain verbs
  (좋아하다, 싫어하다, 시작하다, 쉽다/어렵다) — e.g. 노래하기를 좋아해요, not 노래함을 좋아해요.
- Some (으)ㅁ nominalizations have become independent nouns with drifted meaning (죽다 → 죽음
  "death", 살다 → 삶 "life", 믿다 → 믿음 "belief/faith").
### Examples
* 참가 신청서 제출함.
	* Application form submitted. (formal notice style)
* 이곳에 쓰레기를 버리지 마시기 바람.
	* Please refrain from disposing of trash here. (formal notice)
* 그의 갑작스러운 죽음에 모두 놀랐어요.
	* Everyone was shocked by his sudden death.
* 인생은 짧고 예술은 길다 — 삶(살다의 명사형)과 관련된 표현이 많아요.
	* Life is short, art is long — there are many expressions related to 삶 (the nominalized form of 살다).
### Notes
See [[V는 것이]] and [[Nominalization (todo)]] for the more common everyday -는 것/기 nominalization
approach; (으)ㅁ is comparatively rarer in casual speech but common in formal/written registers.
?end
