## (으)려고 #card
?begin
### Usage
Expresses intention — "in order to" or "planning/intending to" do something. The first clause states
the goal/intention, and the second clause is usually the action taken toward that goal (or the intention itself, when used with 하다).
### Form
| Verb stem ends in vowel | Verb stem ends in consonant |
| --- | --- |
| -려고 | -으려고 |
가다 → 가려고, 먹다 → 먹으려고
### Restrictions
- The subject of both clauses must be the same.
- Cannot be followed directly by an imperative or propositive ending in the same clause (그 자체로 명령/청유형 못 씀).
- Often paired with 하다: -(으)려고 하다 = "to intend/plan to do".
### Examples
* 한국어를 배우려고 학원에 다녀요.
	* I attend a language institute in order to learn Korean.
* 살을 빼려고 운동을 시작했어요.
	* I started exercising in order to lose weight.
* 내일 일찍 일어나려고 알람을 맞췄어요.
	* I set an alarm to wake up early tomorrow.
* 이사하려고 해요.
	* I'm planning to move.
### Notes
Compare with [[(으)러 (가다·오다)]] which is restricted to movement verbs, and [[도록]] which
expresses purpose from the perspective of causing/enabling another subject to do something.
?end
