## (으)면 #card
?begin
### Usage
The most basic conditional connective — "if". States a condition in the first clause; the second
clause states what happens/is true/should be done when that condition is met. Can also express
a general habitual/repeated condition ("whenever").
### Form
| Verb/adj. stem ends in vowel or ㄹ | Ends in other consonant |
| --- | --- |
| -면 | -으면 |
가다 → 가면, 먹다 → 먹으면, 살다 → 살면 (ㄹ drops before ㄹ-initial endings elsewhere, but 면 itself keeps it)
### Restrictions
- Past tense can be inserted before 면 for a past hypothetical: -았/었으면 ("if ... had ...").
- Unlike 거든, (으)면 has no restriction on being used with declaratives, questions, commands, or
  suggestions in the second clause.
### Examples
* 비가 오면 집에 있을 거예요.
	* If it rains, I'll stay home.
* 시간이 있으면 같이 가요.
	* If you have time, let's go together.
* 돈이 많으면 뭐 하고 싶어요?
	* If you had a lot of money, what would you want to do?
* 겨울이 오면 눈이 많이 와요.
	* Whenever winter comes, it snows a lot. (habitual/general)
* 이 약을 먹었으면 좋아졌을 거예요.
	* If you had taken this medicine, you would have gotten better.
### Notes
This is the foundation for many of the more nuanced conditionals in this vault — compare with
[[거든]] (conditional with a "should that happen" nuance and restriction to non-past main clauses),
[[더라면]]/[[더라도]] (retrospective conditionals), and [[다면, N(이)라면]] (hypothetical framing).
?end
