## 안/못/지 않다/지 못하다 (negation) #card
?begin
### Usage
Basic negation comes in two flavors, each with a short (prefix) form and a long (suffix) form:
1. **Simple negation** ("not", the action doesn't happen / isn't true, regardless of ability):
   안 + verb, or Verb stem + 지 않다.
2. **Incapability negation** ("can't", unable to due to circumstance or lack of skill):
   못 + verb, or Verb stem + 지 못하다.
### Form
| Type | Short form | Long form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple | 안 + Verb | Verb stem + 지 않다 |
| Incapability | 못 + Verb | Verb stem + 지 못하다 |

가다 → 안 가요 / 가지 않아요 ; 못 가요 / 가지 못해요
### Restrictions
- 안/못 cannot be inserted directly before "하다" in Noun+하다 verbs when the noun+하다 is
  perceived as a single unit for certain fixed expressions — instead insert between noun and 하다:
  공부 안 해요 (not 안 공부해요, though the latter is heard colloquially for some verbs).
- 못 implies the subject wants to but cannot (external circumstance, lack of skill, etc.), while 안
  implies a simple choice/fact of not doing it.
- Descriptive verbs (adjectives) can use 안/지 않다 but not 못/지 못하다 (since capability doesn't
  apply to states): 안 예뻐요 (O), 못 예뻐요 (X).
### Examples
#### 안 / 지 않다
* 저는 커피를 안 마셔요.
	* I don't drink coffee. (choice)
* 오늘 날씨가 좋지 않아요.
	* The weather isn't good today.
#### 못 / 지 못하다
* 어제 아파서 학교에 못 갔어요.
	* I couldn't go to school yesterday because I was sick.
* 시끄러워서 잠을 자지 못했어요.
	* I couldn't sleep because it was loud.
### Notes
For prohibiting someone from doing something (a command), use -지 마세요 (don't do X), which is
distinct from these fact-stating negations.
?end
