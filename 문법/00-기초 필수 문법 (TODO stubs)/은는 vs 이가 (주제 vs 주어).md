## 은/는 vs 이/가 #card
?begin
### Usage
Both are used to introduce the grammatical subject of a sentence, but they serve different discourse functions:
- **이/가** (subject marker): marks new information, or emphasizes/singles out the subject specifically (answers "who/what?"). Often used the first time something is introduced.
- **은/는** (topic marker): marks the topic of the sentence — what the sentence is "about". Used for contrast, already-known/given information, or general statements/truths.
### Form
| Ends in vowel | Ends in consonant |
| --- | --- |
| 이 | 가 |
| 은 | 는 |
### Restrictions
- In questions asking "who/what is it", 이/가 is usually used in the answer if it's new info.
- In subordinate clauses, 이/가 is almost always required (은/는 rarely appears in embedded clauses).
- 는 can also mark the object or other non-subject elements when contrasting ("그건 안 먹는데 이건 먹어요" — I don't eat that, but I do eat this).
### Examples
#### New information / specific subject (이/가)
* 누가 왔어요? 철수가 왔어요.
	* Who came? Cheolsu came. (answering a "who" question — new info)
* 저기 고양이가 있어요.
	* There's a cat over there. (introducing something for the first time)
#### Topic / contrast / known information (은/는)
* 저는 학생이에요.
	* I am a student. (introducing yourself as the topic)
* 철수는 왔는데 영희는 안 왔어요.
	* Cheolsu came, but Younghee didn't. (contrast)
* 한국어는 재미있어요.
	* Korean (as a topic/general statement) is fun.
### Notes
A classic test: if you can restate the sentence as "Speaking of X, ..." then 은/는 usually fits;
if it directly answers "who/what did/is it?", 이/가 usually fits.
?end
