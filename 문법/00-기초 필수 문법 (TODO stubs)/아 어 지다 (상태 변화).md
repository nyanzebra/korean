## 아/어지다 (state change) #card
?begin
### Usage
Attached to a descriptive verb (adjective) stem, expresses a gradual change into that state —
"to become [adjective]". This is distinct from the passive-voice use of -아/어지다 with action verbs
(already covered in [[passive]]), which instead means "to become done/to get [verb]ed".
### Form
| Stem vowel | Ending |
| --- | --- |
| ㅏ, ㅗ | -아지다 |
| other vowels | -어지다 |
| 하다 (as -하다 adjectives) | -해지다 |
예쁘다 → 예뻐지다, 따뜻하다 → 따뜻해지다, 좋다 → 좋아지다
### Restrictions
- With adjectives, -아/어지다 always means "become," never "passive" (since adjectives have no
  action to be "done to" them by someone else).
- With action verbs instead, -아/어지다 forms the passive voice (see [[passive]]) — e.g. 만들다 →
  만들어지다 ("to be made"), not "to become making."
### Examples
* 날씨가 점점 따뜻해지고 있어요.
	* The weather is gradually getting warmer.
* 요즘 한국어 실력이 많이 좋아졌어요.
	* My Korean skills have improved a lot recently.
* 헤어진 후에 얼굴이 안 좋아졌어요.
	* After the breakup, (his/her) face/expression got worse (looked unwell).
* 화장을 하니까 훨씬 예뻐졌어요.
	* After putting on makeup, (she) became much prettier.
* 이 노래를 들으면 슬퍼져요.
	* Whenever I listen to this song, I become sad.
### Notes
Rule of thumb: adjective + 아/어지다 = "become ___" (state change); action verb + 아/어지다 = passive
voice, "get ___ed" (see [[passive]] for the full passive-verb treatment, including the dedicated
피동사 forms like 보이다/먹히다/잡히다 that are preferred over 아/어지다 when they exist).
?end
