## 아서/어서 (basic) #card
?begin
### Usage
The most fundamental connective ending, with two related meanings:
1. **Reason/cause** — "because", for an unplanned/natural result (no volition implied in the connection itself).
2. **Sequence** — "and then", when the two actions happen in order and are related (often the first enables the second).
### Form
| Verb/adj. stem vowel | Ending |
| --- | --- |
| ㅏ, ㅗ | -아서 |
| other vowels | -어서 |
| 하다 | -해서 |
No tense marker (-았/었-) goes directly before 아서/어서 in the reason/sequence usage — tense is
expressed only in the final clause.
### Restrictions
- Cannot be used with the imperative (-세요) or propositive (-읍시다) in the following clause when
  expressing reason (use [[-기 때문에]] or [[으니까]] instead for commands/suggestions).
- 아서/어서 implies a natural, non-volitional connection between clauses — for a reason clearly
  chosen/decided by the speaker, [[으니까]] is often more natural before a command or suggestion.
### Examples
#### Reason
* 배가 고파서 밥을 먹었어요.
	* I was hungry, so I ate.
* 길이 막혀서 늦었어요.
	* The road was jammed, so I was late.
#### Sequence
* 친구를 만나서 영화를 봤어요.
	* I met my friend and (then) watched a movie.
* 아침에 일어나서 세수를 해요.
	* I get up in the morning and wash my face.
### Notes
Compare with [[여(서)]] (a related/overlapping form) and [[서 vs 으니까]] (a deeper comparison of
아서/어서 vs 으니까 as reason connectives).
