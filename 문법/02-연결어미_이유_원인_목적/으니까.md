## (으)니까 #card
?begin
### Usage
A connective ending that gives a **reason/justification** for what follows — especially when the next clause is a command, suggestion, promise or intention (where [[아서_어서]] cannot be used). The emphasis is on the reason (the first clause), and the reason is often something the speaker has noticed or the listener already knows.

Three related uses:
1. **Reason before a command/suggestion/intention**: "Since ~, please ~ / let's ~ / I will ~".
2. **Discovery**: "When I did ~, I found that ~" (the first clause is an action, the second is a newly discovered fact in the past tense).
3. **Sentence ending -(으)니까요**: giving a reason in answer to a question ("because ~"), often with a slightly emphatic or explanatory tone. In casual speech: -(으)니깐.
### Form
| Stem | Ending | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ends in vowel | -니까 | 가다 → 가니까, 바쁘다 → 바쁘니까 |
| ends in consonant | -으니까 | 먹다 → 먹으니까, 좋다 → 좋으니까 |
| ㄹ stem (ㄹ drops) | -니까 | 살다 → 사니까, 멀다 → 머니까 |
| ㅂ irregular | -우니까 | 춥다 → 추우니까, 덥다 → 더우니까 |
| ㄷ irregular | -으니까 | 듣다 → 들으니까 |
| noun + 이다 | N(이)니까 | 학생이니까, 친구니까 |
| 하다 | 하니까 | 공부하니까 |
| past | -았/었으니까 | 갔으니까, 먹었으니까 |
### Restrictions
- Can be freely followed by commands (-(으)세요), suggestions (-자, -ㅂ시다, -ㄹ까요?), and intentions (-겠-, -ㄹ게요) — unlike 아서/어서.
- Not used for greetings, thanks, apologies and emotional statements: use 아서/어서 (만나서 반갑습니다, 늦어서 죄송합니다, 초대해 주셔서 감사합니다; ×만나니까 반갑습니다).
- Can take a past-tense marker before it (비가 왔으니까), unlike 아서/어서.
- For plain "cause → natural result" narration of past events (비가 와서 늦었어요), 아서/어서 is more natural; -(으)니까 there sounds as if you are justifying yourself.
- In the "discovery" meaning, the second clause is usually in the past tense and is something newly noticed (먹어 보니까 맛있었어요 → see [[다가 보니까_고 보니_아 보니까_다가 보면_고 보면]]).
### Examples
* 오늘은 추우니까 따뜻하게 입으세요.
	* It's cold today, so please dress warmly.
* 시간이 없으니까 택시를 탑시다.
	* We don't have time, so let's take a taxi.
* 내가 도와줄 테니까 걱정하지 마. (see [[을 테니까]])
	* I'll help you, so don't worry.
* 어제 이 영화를 봤으니까 다른 영화를 볼까요?
	* I watched this movie yesterday, so shall we watch a different one?
* 저는 학생이니까 학생 할인을 받을 수 있어요.
	* I'm a student, so I can get the student discount.
* 문을 여니까 바람이 들어왔어요.
	* When I opened the door, wind came in. (discovery meaning; 열다 is an ㄹ stem → 여니까)
* 집에 가 보니까 아무도 없었어요.
	* When I went home (and looked), nobody was there.
* A: 왜 안 가요? B: 비가 오니까요.
	* A: Why aren't you going? B: Because it's raining.
### Notes
- **아서/어서 vs 으니까**: 아서/어서 = natural cause and effect, emphasis on the result, no commands/suggestions, no tense before it. -(으)니까 = justification, emphasis on the reason, allows commands/suggestions and tense. Full comparison with many examples: [[서 vs 으니까]]. Basic card: [[아서_어서]]; related [[여서]], [[라서]].
- 으니까 also appears in -(으)ㄹ 테니까 ([[을 테니까]]), which is -(으)ㄹ 테 ("supposition/intention") + -(으)니까.
- Other reason endings: [[-기 때문에]] (formal/written, no commands), [[고 해서]], [[는 바람에]], [[는 탓에]].
