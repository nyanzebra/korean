## 조차 & 마저 & 까지도 #card
?begin
### Usage
All three mean "even," adding an extreme or unexpected case on top of others already assumed. Nuance differences:
- **조차** — "even" in a negative context; implies something so basic/minimal that its failure is surprising or severe. Almost always with negative predicates.
- **마저** — "even (this) last remaining thing too" — implies this is the final straw, often with a regretful/pessimistic tone (something already bad gets worse).
- **까지도** — more neutral "even up to X too," simple emphasis without the negative or "last straw" connotations of the other two.
### Form
Noun + 조차 / 마저 / 까지도
### Restrictions
- 조차 is almost always used with negative statements ("not even X").
- 마저 typically implies a sequence where other things already failed/left, and now this last thing does too.
- 까지도 can be used in both positive and negative sentences.
### Examples
#### 조차 — "not even"
* 그는 이름조차 기억 못해요.
	* He doesn't even remember the name.
* 밥 먹을 시간조차 없어요.
	* I don't even have time to eat.
#### 마저 — "even this last one too"
* 친구들도 다 떠났는데 너마저 나를 떠나는구나.
	* All my friends already left, and now even you are leaving me too.
* 돈도 없는데 건강마저 잃었어요.
	* I have no money, and now I've lost my health too.
#### 까지도 — neutral "even"
* 아이까지도 그 문제를 풀 수 있어요.
	* Even a child can solve that problem.
* 사장님까지도 참석하셨어요.
	* Even the boss attended.
### Notes
Rough intensity ranking for "even" in negative contexts: 도 < 까지도 < 조차 < 마저 (마저 carries the
strongest sense of "the last/worst thing").
?end
