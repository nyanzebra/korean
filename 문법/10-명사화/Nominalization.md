## Nominalization: -기, -(으)ㅁ, -는 것 #card
?begin
### Usage
Korean has three common ways to turn a verb/adjective into a noun-like phrase ("doing ~", "the fact that ~"):
1. **-기** — the general, abstract/unrealised "act of ~ing" (the *idea* of the action). Very common in set patterns (-기 전에, -기 때문에, -기로 하다, -기 바라다).
2. **-(으)ㅁ** — a formal/written nominaliser for facts, results and states. Used in notices, headlines, reports, diaries, and in many nouns that have become fixed words (죽음, 삶, 믿음, 꿈, 기쁨).
3. **-는 것** — the all-purpose, spoken nominaliser ("the thing of ~ing"); concrete/actual events and perceived facts. Colloquially shortened to **-는 거**.

### Form
| Pattern | Verb | Adjective | Past | Future/prospective |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| -기 | 가기, 먹기 | 크기 (being big) | (-았/었기: 갔기 때문에) | – |
| -(으)ㅁ | 감, 먹음, 삶 (ㄹ stem + ㅁ) | 큼, 좋음 | 갔음, 먹었음 | – |
| -는 것 | 가는 것, 먹는 것 | 큰 것, 좋은 것 | 간 것, 먹은 것 | 갈 것, 먹을 것 |

- -기: attach directly to the stem — no batchim rule.
- -(으)ㅁ: vowel/ㄹ stem + ㅁ, consonant stem + 음 (see [[으ㅁ]]).
- -는 것: action verb + 는 것; adjective/이다 + (으)ㄴ 것; past -(으)ㄴ 것; future/guess -(으)ㄹ 것. 있다/없다 → 있는 것/없는 것.
- 것 takes particles like any noun: 하는 것이 / 하는 것을 / 하는 것은 / 하는 것도 (often contracted in speech: 하는 게, 하는 걸, 하는 건).

### Restrictions
- **-기** is required in fixed patterns: -기 전에, -기 때문에, -기 위해(서), -기로 하다, -기 바라다, -기 쉽다/어렵다/힘들다, -기 시작하다, -기 싫다, -기 마련이다. It is also natural with 좋아하다/싫어하다 (노래하기를 좋아해요; 노래하는 것을 좋아해요 is also fine).
- **-기** is not used with verbs of direct perception (보다, 듣다, 느끼다) — use -는 것: 그가 노래하는 것을 들었어요 (not ~~노래하기를 들었어요~~).
- **-(으)ㅁ** sounds formal/written; it is rare in casual speech. Common with 알다/모르다/밝히다/느끼다 in written style (… 사실을 알게 됨), and as a sentence-ending in notices and notes (확인함, 완료됨, 없음).
- Several -(으)ㅁ forms are now independent nouns with drifted meaning: 죽음 (death), 삶 (life), 믿음 (belief), 꿈 (dream), 기쁨 (joy), 슬픔 (sadness). Do not freely coin new ones in speech.
- **-는 것** is the safest choice when unsure, but in set patterns the -기 form is mandatory (×하는 것 전에, ×하는 것 때문에).
- **-는 것** can be followed by 이다 to define/explain: 제 취미는 책을 읽는 것이에요. -기 also works here (제 취미는 책 읽기예요) but sounds more like a "title".

### Examples
* 한국어는 배우기 어려워요.
	* Korean is hard to learn. (-기 + adjective of difficulty)
* 저는 영화 보기를 좋아해요.
	* I like watching movies. (-기 with 좋아하다)
* 그 사람이 노래하는 것을 들었어요.
	* I heard that person singing. (-는 것 with perception verb)
* 건강을 지키는 것이 가장 중요해요.
	* Keeping your health is the most important thing. (-는 것 as subject)
* 제 취미는 사진을 찍는 거예요.
	* My hobby is taking photos. (spoken contraction 거 = 것)
* 그가 거짓말을 했음이 밝혀졌어요.
	* It was revealed that he had lied. (-(으)ㅁ, formal/written)
* 내일 회의에 참석하시기 바랍니다.
	* Please attend tomorrow's meeting. (-기 바라다, formal request)
* 영수가 벌써 집에 간 것을 몰랐어요.
	* I didn't know that Youngsu had already gone home. (past -(으)ㄴ 것)
* 신분증 지참함.
	* ID brought. (-(으)ㅁ in a note/form)
* 그의 죽음은 모두에게 큰 슬픔이었어요.
	* His death was a great sorrow to everyone. (lexicalised -(으)ㅁ nouns)

### Notes
- Quick rule: **-기** = abstract/pattern-bound; **-(으)ㅁ** = formal/written/fixed noun; **-는 것** = concrete/spoken, can follow tense (간 것, 갈 것).
- Related: [[V는 것이]] (-ㄴ/는다는 것이 "I meant to ~ but ~") and [[으ㅁ]] (full -(으)ㅁ card).
- 3-way contrast: 운동하기는 쉽지 않아요 (the idea of exercising) / 운동하는 것은 쉽지 않아요 (actually doing it) / 운동함 (note-style, formal).
